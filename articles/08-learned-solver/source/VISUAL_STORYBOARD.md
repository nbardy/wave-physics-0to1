# Visual Article Proposal — **The Learned Solver**

## Verdict

Build this as a **sequel to Lesson 01**, not a rewrite.

Lesson 01 already teaches the reader how to see a velocity field, earns advection,
viscosity, pressure, incompressibility, and then exposes the Stable Fluids timestep.
The new article should reuse that mental palette and ask a new question:

> A numerical solver is already a stack of approximations. Which layer should a
> neural network learn, and what must remain verifiable?

A working title is **The Learned Solver**. A more explicit alternative is
**Where Deep Learning Enters Navier–Stokes**.

Recommended placement: `waves` lesson 04 or 05, depending on whether the banked
3-D drag/turbulence lesson goes first.

## The page-level visual invention

Add a persistent, compact **equation dock** near the top of the viewport:

```text
∂u/∂t = −(u·∇)u − (1/ρ)∇p + ν∇²u + f     ∇·u = 0
```

Every term is a focus target. Hovering or tapping one term changes all nearby
figures in concert:

- the physical phenomenon is emphasized;
- the numerical operation that approximates it is emphasized;
- the dominant error source is emphasized;
- the deep-learning insertion point is emphasized;
- the acceptance test is emphasized.

The equation therefore becomes the article's navigation spine rather than a
display equation the reader passes once.

## Visual encoding

Preserve the repo's existing quantity colors exactly:

- velocity and advection: blue;
- viscosity and smoothing: green;
- pressure: red / cyan;
- divergence, residual, or physical failure: violet;
- boundaries and geometry: gray;
- watched parcels or measurements: amber.

Do **not** give “AI” a competing rainbow color. Encode layers by shape instead:

- solid colored border: physical term or classical numerical operation;
- dashed dark border with a faint dot grid: learned approximation;
- violet meter or overlay: residual, divergence, uncertainty, or failure;
- green gate: accepted result;
- gray branch: fallback or escalation.

That lets color keep meaning “what physical thing is this?” while line style
answers “who computed it?”

## Story spine

### 1. The finished machine, with a new IOU

Return to the Lesson 01 wing or cylinder. Beside the running flow, expose a compact
timestep strip:

```text
force → diffuse / close → advect → boundaries → pressure projection
```

The reader can hover each stage and see the corresponding equation term glow.

Plant the debt: some steps are wrong because the grid is coarse, some because the
physics is unresolved, and some are merely expensive. A neural network should not
be offered the same job in all three cases.

### 2. Continuum law versus numerical machine

Dual pane:

- left: the continuous equation over a smooth field;
- right: cells, stencils, interpolation, sparse matrices, and tolerances.

One interaction changes grid size. The equation does not change; its numerical
representation does.

### 3. The error budget

Use `03_error_budget` as the static scaffold, then make each row interactive:

- model-form error;
- discretization error;
- iterative error;
- input/state error;
- tail and decision error.

The article's key sentence belongs here:

> Before asking whether learning improves a solver, name the error it is supposed
> to reduce.

### 4. Learned advection

Reuse the conceptual shape of `AdvectionSchemes`:

- conventional coarse-grid update;
- a learned flux or residual correction;
- a fine-grid reference ghost.

The knob is grid resolution. The meter is mass error plus field error. The learned
method is allowed to win only where those meters support the claim.

### 5. Learned pressure acceleration

This should be the first **real trained model** in the article because it has a
clean safety contract.

Pipeline:

```text
divergence b
  → tiny convolutional network predicts p₀
  → PCG / multigrid continues
  → accept only when ‖Ap − b‖ < ε
```

A bad model costs iterations, not correctness.

Interactive figure:

- same frozen divergence field;
- left solver starts from zero;
- right solver starts from the network;
- one shared residual tolerance;
- two iteration counters;
- both final pressure fields must agree within tolerance.

Training can be done offline from divergence/pressure pairs generated by the
existing solver. Export fixed weights to TypeScript or WGSL. The browser demo runs
real inference and the real classical finish.

### 6. Learned closure

Use a coarse and fine wake simultaneously.

The fine simulation supplies a filtered unresolved stress. The learned closure
predicts a stress or force term, not the next image. Show three panes:

- coarse solver without closure;
- coarse solver with learned closure;
- fine reference.

Meters:

- force or drag;
- wake frequency;
- resolved kinetic energy;
- conservation defect.

This is scientifically richer than the pressure demo, but it should come later
because generalization and rollout stability are much harder.

### 7. The whole-solver surrogate

Put a dashed wrapper around the entire timestep. The input becomes geometry and
forcing; the output becomes a future field or quantity of interest.

The interaction changes one parameter inside and then outside the training range.
Inside, the surrogate is fast and close. Outside, the uncertainty or disagreement
meter should rise and the page should escalate to the classical solver.

### 8. The same move across four fields

Use `04_cross_field_hybrids`:

- fluids: learn closure or numerical acceleration;
- weather: learn parameterization or the full forecast operator;
- molecular dynamics: learn the potential-energy surface;
- solid mechanics: learn the constitutive law.

The mapping between fields is the lesson: each keeps different structure fixed and
learns a different missing map.

### 9. Physics-informed does not mean verified

Playable failure:

- soft conservation penalty;
- one-step training;
- rollout;
- mass or energy slowly drifts violet.

Then introduce hard projection, solver-in-the-loop training, uncertainty, and
fallback as repairs.

### 10. The verified hybrid

Final architecture:

```text
learned proposal
  → symmetry / conservation structure
  → numerical residual correction
  → uncertainty and applicability gate
  → accept evidence or escalate fidelity
```

Return to the opening wing or wave. The reader can now turn on learning at
individual seams and see exactly which contract protects each seam.

## Reuse before invention

Existing pieces that should be reused or adapted:

- `TermToggle`: equation surgery;
- `SolverXray`: timestep stages;
- `JacobiRelax`: pressure iteration;
- `AdvectionSchemes`: side-by-side numerical disagreement;
- `FluidSolver` / `FluidSolverGPU`: source of honest training and evaluation data;
- `PALETTE`: quantity identity;
- the headless figure-audit pattern.

Do not put a generic flowchart library into the page. The strongest figures should
remain bespoke SVG/canvas components under the same `Sim` shell.

## New figure-audit obligations

For every learned figure, test the claim rather than its pixels:

- the learned prediction actually changes when the input changes;
- residual or conservation meters move with the relevant knob;
- both pressure paths end below the same tolerance;
- the warm start reduces iterations on held-out cases;
- an out-of-distribution case triggers uncertainty or fallback;
- the closure improves the named quantity of interest, not merely field RMSE;
- the fallback produces the classical accepted result;
- no model is labeled as trained unless the shipped weights and training manifest
  exist in the repository.

## Recommended first implementation slice

Build the equation dock, the four static diagrams, and the pressure-warm-start
experiment first.

That slice is enough to establish the complete visual grammar:

- equation term;
- numerical operation;
- learned proposal;
- residual gate;
- accepted result.

It is also small enough to be honest. The pressure network cannot quietly rewrite
the physics, because the existing solver still owns the final residual.
