## Core

Einstein and Grossmann developed metric gravity while still struggling to find
its field equations. Distinguish coordinate labels, metric measurements,
connection, curvature, and the law selecting a metric. Their 1913 Entwurf was a
serious theory with failures; its failure was not inevitable or necessary.

## Sequence and experiments

1. Metric: one circular arc, Cartesian/polar coordinates, same measured length;
   radius changes both pictures. Distinguish variable coefficients from curvature.
2. Connection/curvature: tangent-vector transport on flat/spherical triangles;
   change spherical longitude span, measure returned rotation. Surface analogy is
   explicitly separate from spacetime gravity.
3. Falling frames: two particles in uniform and linear-gradient fields; scrub time,
   compare separation. Derive relative acceleration and relate to tidal curvature.
4. History and field equation: Grossmann's substantive role, notebook search,
   Newtonian limit and coordinate difficulties, no fabricated dialogue or causal myth.
5. Mercury: integrate Newton and Schwarzschild orbit equation from matched ICs,
   amplify compactness visibly; numerical apsidal readout separate from physical
   leading-order43″/century calculation. Historical18″ is cited, not simulated.
