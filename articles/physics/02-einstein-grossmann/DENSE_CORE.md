# DENSE CORE — The Draft That Had to Fail (Einstein–Grossmann, 1912–1915)

The compressed inspiration. Written first; wins conflicts with later drafts.

## Thesis

Gravity is not a pull. It is the shape of the ruler-field everything moves
through — and the man who saw that clearly could not write it down until his
classmate handed him the mathematics. Their joint draft got the shape right
and the law wrong, and the wrongness was load-bearing: it named the two
tests — fall like Newton, bend like no one — that the right law had to pass.

## Hook

Cold open on the wonder gap: every reader has watched things fall straight
down, and no one has seen the ground they fall through. Then the human beat,
stated flat: in August 1912 Einstein walked across Zurich to Grossmann's
office with the physics done and the mathematics missing, and Grossmann
answered with Riemann, Ricci, and Levi-Civita. Then the hero: two nearby
"straight lines," drawn on the flat page and on the curved protagonist —
on one they stay apart, on the other they meet. Flat declarative IOU, never
promissory: the top lines are the geometry you were given; the bottom lines
are the geometry you live in.

## Payoff

The reader performs the three acts, not reads them:

1. They draw a "straight line" on a curved surface and watch it refuse to
   stay straight by the flat meaning of the word — the ordinary derivative
   dies on the curved sheet.
2. They lay the repair — the metric, then its connection — and drive the
   triangle's angles and the loop's holonomy to the values curvature dictates.
3. They run the two laws against the two tests: Newton's fall and Mercury's
   43″. The 1913 draft passes the first and scores 18″ on the second; the
   November 1915 law scores 43″. The hero returns, readable: falling is
   going straight through curved geometry.

Closing benediction: the next time something drops, there is no hand pulling
it — there is a geometry, and the thing is going straight.

## Ranked insights

1. **The division of labor is the story.** Einstein owned the physical demand
   (gravity = spacetime geometry, motion = geodesics, Newton must fall out);
   Grossmann owned the machinery (the metric, the absolute differential
   calculus of Ricci and Levi-Civita). The 1913 paper signs it that way:
   Einstein the physical part, Grossmann the mathematical part.
2. **Right shape, wrong law.** The draft's geometry — metric field, geodesic
   motion — survives into the final theory untouched. What dies is the field
   law: equations covariant only under a restricted group, kept because they
   seemed to protect energy-momentum and the Newtonian limit.
3. **A wrong theory that grades itself.** The Einstein–Besso manuscript
   (June 1913) runs the draft against Mercury and gets 18″ of the observed
   43″ per century. The failure is quantitative, on the page, two years
   before the fix — the draft convicts itself with a number.
4. **Covariance is physics, not notation.** "Works in every coordinate system"
   sounds like bookkeeping until the draft's restricted covariance visibly
   breaks the fall of a planet. General covariance earns its keep on the
   Mercury meter.
5. **The friend is load-bearing.** Grossmann introduced the tradition in
   August 1912 (Riemann, Ricci, Levi-Civita), co-signed the 1913 Outline,
   presented it with Einstein on 9 September 1913. Lone-genius framings of
   1915 cannot survive the Zurich notebook of 1912–13, which documents the
   search page by page.
6. **November 1915 is four weeks, not one stroke.** Field equations across
   the 4th, 11th, 18th, and 25th communiques to the Prussian Academy; Mercury
   (43″) lands on the 18th using the techniques built with Besso in 1913.
   The draft's tools close the case against it.

## Misconception kill-list

- Debunk flatly: lone-genius 1915 (the paper trail — notebook, joint paper,
  joint presentation — kills it); "the math was just formalism" (without the
  Ricci calculus there is no object to hang the physics on).
- Keep distinct: shape vs. law (the draft's geometry survives; its field
  equations don't — one explicit sentence, or the reader complects them);
  restricted vs. general covariance (continuous, testable, meter-visible).
- Omit entirely: general tensor calculus pedagogy beyond the two objects we
  touch (the metric, one connection); Schwarzschild/exact solutions; black
  holes and cosmology (named-not-built in Further Reading).
- Confess-and-retire: every curved-surface figure is spatial curvature as a
  stand-in for spacetime curvature — said once, up front, in I-voice; time
  joins honestly only at the Mercury figure.

## What this article is NOT

- Not a biography of either man. Dates present (August 1912, 1913, June
  1913, September 1913, November 1915, 1859) are load-bearing for specific
  claims, not a chronology.
- Not a GR textbook. One law is earned (go straight through curved
  geometry); the field equation arrives words-first, symbols-last, and only
  the two terms the meters already touched get symbols.
- No `<Q>` second-voice layer (AGENTS decision, 2026-07-06).
