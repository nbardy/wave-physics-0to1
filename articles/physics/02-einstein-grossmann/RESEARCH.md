# RESEARCH — Einstein–Grossmann (verified sources only)

Every historical beat in the article traces to an entry here. DENSE_CORE wins
conflicts with later drafts; this document wins conflicts about facts.

## 1. The collaboration and the division of labor

- Einstein returned to ETH Zurich in 1912 and visited his old classmate
  Marcel Grossmann (professor of mathematics), who introduced him to
  Riemannian geometry and differential geometry generally.
  Source: [History of general relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_general_relativity)
  (recommendation of Levi-Civita for general covariance noted there).
- Einstein's own recall (Kyoto 1922 address): he had grasped the decisive
  idea — the analogy between extending relativity to accelerated motion and
  Gauss's theory of surfaces — "without being aware at that time of the work
  of Riemann, Ricci, and Levi-Civita"; Grossmann introduced him to that
  tradition in August 1912.
  Source: secondary confirmation via
  [The Forgotten Tradition (Philsci Archive)](https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/10160/1/Forgotten_Tradition_(korrigiert).pdf)
  and [Jordan historical analysis (arXiv:2507.23758)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.23758v1)
  (Ricci/Levi-Civita treatise; Einstein aware only from 1912 via Grossmann).
- The joint paper — Einstein & Grossmann, 1913, "Entwurf einer
  verallgemeinerten Relativitätstheorie und einer Theorie der Gravitation"
  (Outline) — is signed with Einstein responsible for the physical part and
  Grossmann for the mathematical part, and already contains all elements of
  the final theory's kinematics.
  Source: [Genesis of the problem of motion (arXiv:1803.09872)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1803.09872)
  ("signed responsible for the 'physical part' … 'mathematical part'").
- Presented jointly 9 September 1913 to the 96th annual meeting of the
  Schweizerische Naturforschende Gesellschaft.
  Source: [Einstein–Grossmann Outline discussion (arXiv:1312.4068)](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/1312.4068);
  the Outline was given up only in fall 1915 at the final breakthrough.

## 2. The Zurich notebook (summer 1912 – spring 1913)

- The notebook documents Einstein's and Grossmann's joint search; Einstein
  considered field equations close to the final November 1915 ones and set
  them aside on physical grounds (Newtonian limit, energy-momentum), choosing
  the less elegant Entwurf equations instead.
  Sources: [Christie's: first outline of GR](https://onlineonly.christies.com/s/shoulders-giants-making-modern-world/first-outline-general-relativity-15/70042)
  and Tilman Sauer's [working-sheets paper
  (arXiv:1906.06106)](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/1906.06106) (on the later
  unified-field-theory sheets; its footnotes confirm the 1913 precursor
  equations and point to the Zurich Notebook facsimile at the Einstein
  Archives Online, searchable as "Zurich Notebook").

## 3. What the Entwurf got right and wrong

- Right: the gravitational field represented by a metric tensor; particles
  subject only to gravity move on geodesics of that metric (equivalence
  principle realized geometrically).
  Source: [Genesis of the problem of motion](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1803.09872).
- Wrong: non-generally-covariant field equations, chosen via
  energy-momentum considerations instead of the November tensor; they reduce
  to the Newtonian limit but are too restrictive.
  Source: [Einstein and the problem of confirmation (arXiv:2201.00807)](http://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2201.00807).

## 4. Mercury: 18″ (1913) vs 43″ (1915)

- June 1913, Zurich: Einstein and Michele Besso solve the Entwurf equations
  for the Sun's field and compute Mercury's perihelion advance in the
  Einstein–Besso manuscript: **about 18″ per century** against the observed
  ~43″. Einstein kept quiet about it and continued with the Entwurf.
  Sources: [Janssen & Renn on the perihelion (arXiv:2111.11238)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.11238v1.pdf),
  [Einstein's methodology (arXiv:1209.5181)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.5181v1.pdf),
  [commentary on Janssen–Renn (arXiv:2201.00807)](https://export.arxiv.org/abs/2201.00807v1).
- November 1915: Einstein reports to the Prussian Academy (communiques of
  Nov 4, 11, 18, 25); on **18 November 1915** he shows the new theory gives
  **43″ per century**, against the astronomers' 45″ ± 5″ remainder
  (Le Verrier 1859; Newcomb 1882).
  Sources: [Weinstein on Einstein–Schwarzschild–Mercury (arXiv:1411.7370)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.7370),
  [UFL gravity lectures ch. 10](http://www.phys.ufl.edu/~cmw/Gravity-Lectures/Chapter%2010.pdf)
  (Le Verrier 1859 discrepancy ≈ 43″/century).
- The 1915 computation reuses the techniques built with Besso in 1913
  (solving for the Sun's field, then the orbit).
  Source: [arXiv:1201.5353](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1201.5353) (method transfer
  from the 1913 manuscript to the Nov 18, 1915 paper).

## 5. Numbers the article may print

- Entwurf Mercury prediction: **≈ 18″/century** (Einstein–Besso ms.).
- GR Mercury prediction: **43″/century**; contemporary observed remainder
  **45″ ± 5″**.
- Observed anomaly origin: **Le Verrier (1859)**, refined by Newcomb (1882).
- Presentation date: **9 September 1913** (Swiss Natural Science Society).
- Mercury paper date: **18 November 1915** (Prussian Academy).
