## Building the Navier–Stokes Equations

### Corrections

1. **P1: the pressure-cancellation puzzle reverses the force.** [PressureFix.tsx](/Users/nicholasbardy/git/wave_physics_0to1/src/sims/PressureFix.tsx:21) says a pressure hill's negative gradient points inward. A positive Gaussian hill pushes outward. The code nevertheless subtracts an inward correction from an outward provisional flow, while [the prose](/Users/nicholasbardy/git/wave_physics_0to1/src/lessons/lesson-01-navier-stokes.mdx:448) says the hill cancels it. Positive divergence is also described as piling up, reversing the local volume balance. Preserve the hill but start with converging provisional velocity; compute the correction from the actual pressure gradient and shade its actual signed divergence. Treat the initial field as a proposed velocity requiring correction, not a real source that pressure can erase.

2. **P2: the Reynolds similarity example changes Re by four.** [Lines 320–321](/Users/nicholasbardy/git/wave_physics_0to1/src/lessons/lesson-01-navier-stokes.mdx:320) say doubling speed and halving viscosity preserves the wake. With `Re = UL/ν`, it quadruples Re. Double both speed and kinematic viscosity at fixed geometry, and compare the flow after rescaling time.

3. **P2: the material-derivative example needs an externally maintained field.** [ParcelProbe.tsx](/Users/nicholasbardy/git/wave_physics_0to1/src/sims/ParcelProbe.tsx:15) fixes a Gaussian dye concentration in space while water passes through it. This is a valid prescribed scalar field but not freely carried dye without sources and sinks. The next section correctly teaches `Dq/Dt = 0` for passive advection, creating a direct contradiction. Use temperature maintained by heating and cooling, with that condition stated, or use acceleration through a steady narrowing channel. The former is a small repair; the latter connects more directly to Newton's law but needs figure work.

4. **P2: dye diffusion, momentum diffusion, and two viscosities get conflated.** [The advection-to-viscosity transition](/Users/nicholasbardy/git/wave_physics_0to1/src/lessons/lesson-01-navier-stokes.mdx:247) makes viscosity sound like the cause of dye spreading. Distinguish scalar diffusivity from momentum diffusivity, then use their common smoothing operator as the bridge. [The viscosity comparison](/Users/nicholasbardy/git/wave_physics_0to1/src/lessons/lesson-01-navier-stokes.mdx:299) gives dynamic viscosity in mPa·s after introducing kinematic viscosity `ν`; state `ν = μ/ρ`. The mercury comparison and the later air/Re explanation need this distinction.

5. **P2: force is labeled with acceleration units.** [The pressure equation](/Users/nicholasbardy/git/wave_physics_0to1/src/lessons/lesson-01-navier-stokes.mdx:397) calls `−∇p/ρ` the force on a parcel. It is acceleration, or force per unit mass. Relabel it.

6. **P2: the water-wave coda promises too much.** [Lines 597–607](/Users/nicholasbardy/git/wave_physics_0to1/src/lessons/lesson-01-navier-stokes.mdx:597) identify general sea-surface waves with the same nondispersive wave equation as sound. The implementation does solve that equation, but the surface interpretation requires small-amplitude, long shallow-water gravity waves, with capillarity and damping neglected. General gravity waves satisfy `ω² = gk tanh(kh)`. The fixed-horizontal-position “floats” are height markers, not computed material trajectories. Name them accordingly or implement particle motion. [MIT hydrodynamics notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-20-marine-hydrodynamics-13-021-spring-2005/5d48a5937971d973fd8ca90c051a83f8_lecture20.pdf)

7. **P2: the prize framing exceeds the actual mathematical uncertainty.** [The opening](/Users/nicholasbardy/git/wave_physics_0to1/src/lessons/lesson-01-navier-stokes.mdx:29) makes a two-dimensional numerical wing appear to defy an existence question about three-dimensional continuum solutions. “Nobody can prove makes sense” also erases known local smooth and global weak existence results. Specify the relevant global three-dimensional question and distinguish continuum guarantees from finite numerical approximations. This correction is necessary regardless of the new announcement. [Official problem statement](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/navierstokes.pdf)

8. **P2: the numerical explanation has drifted from the implementation.** [Lines 493–498](/Users/nicholasbardy/git/wave_physics_0to1/src/lessons/lesson-01-navier-stokes.mdx:493) describe a rising pressure hill; the relaxation figure's outward seed produces a pressure well. The review reproduced center pressures of −26.4065 and −31.7577 at 40 and 80 sweeps. Its divergence RMS ratios, 0.208669 and 0.109200, support the prose's one-fifth and one-tenth claims. The slider calls the in-place Gauss–Seidel method “Jacobi.” Later blanket statements about forty sweeps and the operation order do not match every figure: the CPU step diffuses before advection, and the GPU pressure solve uses multigrid. Describe the demonstration and the production solver variants separately in a few precise sentences.

Additional qualifications worth folding into those edits: divergence-free does not mean “only whirlpools”; the large background pressure around a fish should not be confused with the hydrostatic gradient balancing gravity; laminar flow can mix, so “an orderly flow does not mix” must be restricted to the displayed geometry; prescribed linear transport can swirl dye, so nonlinearity is not the sole cause of everything that swirls.

### Story recommendations

**Keep the sequence that builds motion, breaks volume conservation, and repairs it with pressure.** That is the article's strongest argument. Make its repaired pressure puzzle the climax, with a simultaneous broken/corrected comparison and a pressure overlay on the same scene. The physics fix and the editorial improvement are the same work.

**Earn Reynolds similarity with two flows that match.** A pair at different speeds and viscosities but matched Re would explain why the ratio matters. Reuse the banked honey/water or dimensionless-comparison concepts. Two live solvers cost more; paired computed snapshots are an alternative if their conditions and times are explicit.

**Consolidate the ending.** The equation assembly, wing return, new wave lesson, and coffee/prize conclusion currently create several finish lines. Let the wing return carry the resolution; shorten or separate the wave coda. This gives up a long curriculum bridge, but the site now has several fields and does not need to make every lesson justify itself through waves.

**Anchor the opening to what the wing actually shows.** The change from clean sheets to an interleaved wake creates a question this article answers. The prize can remain a carefully scoped later boundary on that understanding. This is an editorial preference, not a reason to discard the existing article.

## The History of Navier–Stokes

### Corrections

1. **P1: the Reynolds prediction presents an invented transition as computed physics.** [The prediction](/Users/nicholasbardy/git/wave_physics_0to1/src/lessons/lesson-03-navier-stokes-history.mdx:482) asks what happens when speed doubles. [The implementation](/Users/nicholasbardy/git/wave_physics_0to1/src/sims/ReynoldsTube.tsx:5) explicitly describes an invented growth law with a prescribed threshold of 2000. At line 157, downstream speed remains fixed at `uScale = 0.9`; the slider controls perturbation growth instead. The code and HANDOFF say this is disclosed in prose, but it is not. Disclose the schematic reconstruction, make a speed control actually change transport, and qualify the transition's dependence on disturbances. A two-dimensional cartoon is not experimental or computational evidence for three-dimensional pipe transition. Reynolds's original paper itself discusses the influence of disturbances. [Reynolds, 1883](https://www.homepages.ucl.ac.uk/~uceseug/Fluids3/Extra_Reading/Reynolds_1883.pdf)

2. **P1/P2: the Prandtl payoff needs both a physical correction and the promised visual evidence.** [Lines 528–555](/Users/nicholasbardy/git/wave_physics_0to1/src/lessons/lesson-03-navier-stokes-history.mdx:528) say ideal flow is correct everywhere except a sliver and that nothing pushes on the back. Separation changes an extended wake and the pressure field; the earlier attached potential-flow solution is not correct throughout that outer region. Low rear pressure is not zero rear contribution. The loupe shows a speed wash and local velocity profile, not the disappearing red pressure lobe invoked by the prose. A direct probe using its constants after 400 CPU steps found front `Cp ≈ 1.012`, rear `Cp ≈ −0.233`, and center-wake speed about 0.133 versus far-flow speed 26.887. Preserve the thin-layer insight while showing its extended consequences. Put ideal and viscous pressure maps and force balances beside each other; label the existing meter **pressure drag**, since it omits wall shear. [Prandtl's 1904 paper, translated](https://germanhistory-intersections.org/en/knowledge-and-education/ghis:document-202)

3. **P2: the cascade diagram also lacks its promised disclosure.** [The paragraph after WhorlsCascade](/Users/nicholasbardy/git/wave_physics_0to1/src/lessons/lesson-03-navier-stokes-history.mdx:584) presents energy passing between scales. [The component](/Users/nicholasbardy/git/wave_physics_0to1/src/sims/WhorlsCascade.tsx:5) instead animates prescribed orbits; no energy transfer or conservation is computed. State that it illustrates a proposed three-dimensional cascade. Later “every figure” being two-dimensional should become “our two-dimensional flow solvers”: a flat illustration of 3D turbulence is not a 2D fluid simulation. An actual historical spectrum would support the quantitative −5/3 discussion better than orbiting rings.

4. **P2: the displayed solver uses a collocated grid, not the attributed staggered grid.** [Line 647](/Users/nicholasbardy/git/wave_physics_0to1/src/lessons/lesson-03-navier-stokes-history.mdx:647) claims the displayed steps live on Harlow's staggered grid. [FluidSolver](/Users/nicholasbardy/git/wave_physics_0to1/src/sims/lib/solver.ts:7), used by SolverXray, explicitly uses collocated fields. Keep the historical milestone, describe it as an ancestor, and state this implementation's simplification. A solver rewrite is unnecessary to fix the claim.

5. **P2: Poiseuille's physiological motivation becomes the wrong experimental fluid.** [Lines 454–455](/Users/nicholasbardy/git/wave_physics_0to1/src/lessons/lesson-03-navier-stokes-history.mdx:454) say he measured blood; the waypoint repeats “verified in blood and brass.” His foundational measurements used water, motivated by circulation. Describe that distinction and remove “to the decimal” unless tied to actual measurement accuracy. [Historical study by Sutera and Skalak](https://homepage.ntu.edu.tw/~wttsai/Adv_Fluid/History%20of%20Poiseuilles%20law.pdf)

6. **P2: the coda contradicts the article's own historical explanation.** [Line 673](/Users/nicholasbardy/git/wave_physics_0to1/src/lessons/lesson-03-navier-stokes-history.mdx:673) says Euler answered d'Alembert's absurdity, whereas the Euler section correctly explains that his equations retain the zero-drag paradox. Euler supplied general field equations; Prandtl's work changed how their viscous limit was understood. The next paragraph's claim that every subsequent wave descends from Navier–Stokes also fails for the electromagnetic waves in the fiber-bundles lesson. Restrict the ancestry to appropriate fluid waves, with their required assumptions.

7. **P2: update the mathematical ending precisely.** Besides the current-status issue above, Tao's averaged-equation result limits certain approaches to regularity; it does not establish that no protective structure has ever been written down. Describe that result rather than calling it the strongest evidence against smoothness. [Tao's paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.0290)

Smaller definite repairs: [the closing “six letters”](/Users/nicholasbardy/git/wave_physics_0to1/src/lessons/lesson-03-navier-stokes-history.mdx:704) should be twelve; the calculus-priority paragraph mixes 1687 with the later Royal Society report; “slip survives in one place” is too exclusive. Describe the hero's actual UI: its endpoint is labeled 1999, its open-smoothness plate is separate, and its Reynolds-era cylinder is not the pipe experiment. The permanent blank plate and repeated references to it also need to be coordinated with the dated status note.

### Story recommendations

**Reach the zero-drag prediction sooner.** The current route goes through statics, sound, calculus disputes, and family politics before arriving at its strongest problem. Keep the naming accident, then introduce the competing drag predictions early. Retain background where it equips the reader to understand the next failure. This supports the already-recorded September 4 proposal to organize around the paradox; it is not a newly approved rebuild. The tradeoff is less panoramic history and more sustained attention to one question.

**Make 1904 a visible comparison.** The existing loupe locates local velocity differences; the promised payoff concerns the global force balance. Reusing IdealFlow's force ledger beside the viscous specimen would let the reader see the connection. Prandtl's original suction comparison is a possible further experiment, but it must use authentic historical evidence or a properly implemented boundary condition. It should not become another prescribed animation masquerading as a solve.

**Stop after one principal ending.** Prandtl resolves the drag story, the turbulence/regularity section starts another, the machine-age hero returns, “One Skeleton” recaps, and Final Words repeats the cast. Keep the machine-age return as the main ending and make current mathematics a short dated afterword. This makes the article easier to maintain and gives its strongest payoff more room.

**Let the history remain messier than the teaching order.** The proposed universal pattern—each failed theory repaired by the next term—is contradicted by the article's best examples. Navier reached a useful term through questionable assumptions; Prandtl added no new Navier–Stokes term; numerical methods changed what people could calculate. Organize those differences as writing the law, justifying its assumptions, solving useful cases, and understanding its limits. This replaces the overly tidy thesis with a more informative story.

Preserve the naming accident, Navier's molecular route, the two-sphere race, IdealFlow's computed cancellation, the recurring cylinder, and the qualified front-pressure/back-pressure account. They are the material the revision should build around.
