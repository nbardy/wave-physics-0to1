The five sentences the article, as it stands, implies:

1. A wing sheds a two-coloured braid into its wake, and a slider changes something the
   article declines to name.
2. Then we look at four ways of seeing a flow, then at molecules averaging into arrows.
3. Then we look at a probe against a drifting parcel, then at dye carried by three

5. Then we look at the assembled equation with four switches, at the solver's three stages,

One failure in thirteen sections, arriving at line 407 of 644. Sentence 5 is a label, not a
prediction: the finale re-shows the hero and names the knob; nothing on the page derives
the braid, or its absence, from the equation the reader assembled. The debt the article
opens on is paid by a canvas label at L318 and then ceremonially at L561.

**Hero: the wing.** Familiarity cost: the highest available in this subject. Ciechanowski's
Airfoil is the best-known interactive on the web and this article names it as its
interlocutor; a section shedding a Kármán street is the stock image of every fluid-solver
demo. The wing is at zero tilt and Re 500 and does nothing a wing does — the article's own
verdict is "aerodynamically a moth" — so it is a cylinder in costume, and the costume is
the most familiar one there is.

**Serves or hosts:** neither. The wing appears at L33 and L564 and nowhere between. The
object (the equation) is taught on a rack of unrelated toys — a creek, a particle box, a
one-dimensional jet, a draggable landscape, a prescribed swirl — none of which is the hero.
The specimen is a bookend. No words go to the wing's own physics either; it decorates.

## Part 2 — The new outline

### Hero

**The return.** A channel with a disc in it. The stream has been running; a blob of amber
dye is dropped in upstream and carried past the disc for a few seconds; then the stream
runs backward for the same few seconds. One slider, labelled *honey* at one end and *water*
at the other. At the honey end the blob splits around the disc, stretches, and on the way
back gathers into a blob again, close to where and what it was. At the water end it is
folded into the wake and the reversed stream folds it further; nothing comes back. A small
readout under the canvas prints how much of the original blob the return recovered.

Not yet understandable: the reader can see that honey undoes and water does not and has no
sentence for why, because the naive theory — dye goes where the water goes, so reversing the
water reverses the dye — predicts a return in both.

Returns at the end with the slider's ends relabelled in Reynolds number, and the reader able
to predict, from one line of algebra on the assembled equation, that the return is
near-complete at small Re, gone at large Re, and degrades in between — and to find the
degradation on the slider.

Familiarity cost, honestly: medium. G. I. Taylor's un-mixing film exists and a dye-in-glycerin
version circulates online; a physicist has seen it. Nobody has seen it with viscosity in
their hand, and nobody outside the field has seen the equation forbid it. The Kármán street
is present at the water end but is not the hero; the return is. Lower cost than any obstacle
sweep on offer, and much lower than a wing.

The current hero is not right: it teaches nothing between its two appearances, and the
question it asks (why the braid) the article never answers.

### Thesis

Whether a stir can be undone is decided by one term of the equation — the only term that
keeps its sign when the stream reverses — and how much that term counts, against the term
that drags neighbours together, is the Reynolds number.

### The five sentences

1. A blob of dye carried past a disc by a stream that then runs backward comes back to its
   shape in honey and never in water, and nothing on the screen says why.
2. The obvious fluid is one in which every bit of water keeps its velocity and carries it
   along — Newton with no forces, written with the derivative that rides the fluid — and it
   fails on screen: the stream drives straight into the disc, dye soaks through it, and the
   violet overlay marks fluid being created where none can be.
3. A push that appears wherever fluid is being crowded, tuned by hand and then by the
   machine to exactly the height that cancels the crowding, fixes the heaping and sends the
   blob around the disc — and leaves a fluid that is water at every setting of the slider:
   the honey end is dead, and the blob never returns.
4. A drag between neighbours, one term with one coefficient, gives the slider its honey end
   and brings the blob back — and the same honey fails to bring it back when the stream runs
   three times as fast, so the coefficient alone does not decide.
5. Doubling speed and viscosity together leaves every frame unchanged, so only the ratio
   $UL/\nu$ decides; and in the assembled equation with the stream reversed, three terms
   flip sign and one does not — the carrying term, quadratic in velocity — whose size
   against the viscous term is that ratio, so the return watched at the top is predicted:
   near-complete at small Re, gone at large, degrading between, where the slider, now
   labelled in Re, shows it.

### Section ladder

**0. The return** (hero; intro in Part 3).
Figure: `Return` — NEW. Built on `CylinderFlow`'s channel and solver. Needs: a signed
inflow (when $U<0$ the right edge becomes the fixed inlet and the left the copy-out —
`boundaries()` in `src/sims/lib/solver.ts` and Pass A around line 848 of
`src/sims/lib/gpu/solver_gpu.ts`); a one-shot dye blob (`injectDyeStripe` is a continuous
emitter; a disc stamp is ten lines); a stroke scheduler (pre-roll the stream, drop the
blob, forward $T$, back $T$, hold, restart); an overlap meter
($\int \min(\text{dye}_{\text{end}}, \text{dye}_0) / \int \text{dye}_0$); the slider mapped to
$\nu$ with printed Re only in the finale variant. The honey end is the real cost: a clean
return needs the stroke to be quasi-steady, $UT/D \gg \text{Re}$, so with a stroke of a few
diameters the honey end wants Re $\lesssim 1$, and the implicit diffusion solve's Jacobi
sweep count grows with $\nu\,\Delta t$ — hopeless on the 4× GPU grid at that viscosity. Two
honest routes: a multigrid diffusion solve (the pressure multigrid restrict/prolong kernels
already exist under `src/sims/lib/gpu/`), or run the hero at the 1× grid where Gauss–Seidel
at ~60 sweeps reaches Re ≈ 1 and physical viscosity swamps the carry step's numerical one.
One to two days either way, plus one measurement that must precede any prose claim: overlap
versus Re across the slider, and the overlap ceiling with no disc in the channel (the carry
step blurs the blob on both legs, so even a perfect velocity return reads below 100%). The
water end needs the wake already shedding when the blob is dropped — pre-roll with the
existing kick seed. Knob: honey ↔ water. Math: none.

**1. Two speeds.**
Naive thing: Newton applied at fixed points — the acceleration of the water at a point is
$\partial \mathbf{u}/\partial t$.
Visible failure: the honey stroke, frozen forward, is steady — every fixed arrow holds its
length and direction — and a rider dropped into it visibly speeds up at the disc's
shoulders and slows behind it; the trace pane shows a flat line for the fixed probe and a

**6. The equation.**
Built: the momentum equation in the order the terms were built —
$\dfrac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t} = -(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}
 - \dfrac{1}{\rho}\nabla p + \nu\nabla^2\mathbf{u}$ with $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u} = 0$ —
each term captioned by the section that failed into it. Then the stream reversed:
substitute $\mathbf{u} \to -\mathbf{u}$, $p \to -p$. The time term flips. The pressure term
flips. The viscous term flips. The carrying term, with two $\mathbf{u}$'s in it, does not.
Divide through: the reversed equation is the forward equation with one term's sign wrong,
and that term's size against the viscous one is $U^2/L$ over $\nu U/L^2$, which is Re. A
stream run backward retraces itself exactly when Re is zero and not otherwise; at small Re
the mismatch is small and the return is close; as Re grows the mismatch grows and the
return fails. This is the reader's slider, predicted.

**Ending — the scallop.**
From this article's material and nowhere else. At Re $10^{-5}$ the one term that survives
reversal is a hundred-thousandth of the viscous one, so any motion and its exact reverse
cancel: a scallop that opens slowly and snaps shut in that world ends each cycle where it
began, because closing is opening run backward and the fluid returns it. No microbe swims by
a stroke that is its own reverse; the ones that swim rotate a helix or bend a whip whose
recovery stroke is not the power stroke played back. A bacterium that stops swimming coasts
a fraction of an atom's width. The un-stirring that opened the article and the swimming
that cannot happen are one fact about one term.

## Part 3 — The intro

A blob of dye in glycerin, in the gap between two glass cylinders, is drawn out into a faint
ring when the inner cylinder is turned four times. Turned back four times, the ring draws
back into a blob, close to the shape it started with. In water the same four turns disperse
the dye inside a turn or two, and no number of turns back collects it. Both liquids obey one
equation; between them, one coefficient in it is about a thousand times larger. That
coefficient decides how much one other term counts, and that other term is the difference
between a stir that can be undone and one that cannot. The channel below carries a blob of
dye past a disc for a few seconds, then runs the stream backward for as long. The slider
runs from honey to water.

*Hero figure:* a channel with a disc, an amber blob dropped upstream into a running stream,
carried forward for a few seconds and back for the same, on a loop, with a readout of how
much of the blob returned; the single knob is the slider from honey to water.

**Audit, one line per sentence.**

1. "A blob of dye in glycerin … turned four times." — States: dye in a Couette cell is
   drawn into a thin ring by a few turns of the inner cylinder (Taylor, NCFMF film, 1966,
   replicated in every fluids classroom). Rule 4: opens on a concrete thing. Rule 3: about
   the fluid. Rules 1, 2: no payoff spent, nothing scheduled. Rule 6: a real bench, not
   imagination. Rule 8: "faint ring" is what is seen. Topic-swap fails to survive; delete
   loses the world anchor; a physicist is talking; it states a fact.
2. "Turned back four times, the ring draws back into a blob, close to the shape it started
   with." — States: the reversal reassembles the blob up to diffusion. Rule 8: "close to."
   Rules 1–4, 6 as above. Disputable only by someone who has not seen the film.
3. "In water the same four turns disperse the dye inside a turn or two, and no number of
   turns back collects it." — States: at the same turning rate water is a thousand times
   higher in Re, the cell goes unstable, and the mixing is irreversible. Rule 8: "inside a
   turn or two" is a hedge on a real number. Rules 1–4, 6 pass. A fact; not an effect.
4. "Both liquids obey one equation; between them, one coefficient in it is about a thousand
   times larger." — States: same Navier–Stokes; $\nu_{\text{glycerin}}/\nu_{\text{water}}
   \approx 1.1\times10^{-3} / 1.0\times10^{-6}$ at room temperature. Rule 8: "about a
   thousand"; density also differs and is not claimed to be the only difference. Rule 1:
   does not name the term. Passes the four tests as a checkable fact.
5. "That coefficient decides how much one other term counts, and that other term is the
   difference between a stir that can be undone and one that cannot." — The thesis, a full
   sentence. States: the ratio of the inertial term to the viscous term is what the
   coefficient controls, and reversibility fails through the inertial term. Disputable by an
   expert (and correct). Rule 1: does not say which term or why; the sign count stays unspent.
   Rule 5 (optional): the one quotable, last but for the pointer. Not an effect on the
   reader: it is a claim about the equation.
6. "The channel below carries a blob of dye past a disc for a few seconds, then runs the
   stream backward for as long." — States what the figure does. A camera move on a concrete
   object (the boundary SLOP 20 allows). Rule 2: not a schedule — the figure is now. Rule 9:
   sharpens what the reader is about to see.
7. "The slider runs from honey to water." — States the knob. Rule 8: the ends are labelled
   exactly that. No effect, no mood.

No "imagine." No metaphor needing an audit. No aphorism opens. Shape against the p-bits
