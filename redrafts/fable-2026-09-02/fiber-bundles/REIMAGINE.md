## Part 2 — The new outline

### Hero

One artifact: two rows of clocks, one above the other, with the blue rule
$A_y(x)$ drawn between them — the number, at each position, by which a lower
needle must turn on its way up to its partner to count as pointing the same
way. Every needle at rest. Each upper clock also draws a faint needle: its
partner's, carried up by the rule; with the curve flat, the two coincide.
One interaction: a tap on the curve plucks it. The bump splits, runs both ways
at one speed with distance $= ct$ markers riding the fronts, and the faint
needles swing off and back as it passes, later at each pair.

Build: `ConnectionWave` mode `'pluck'` with one rendering change (NEW, small —
one draw function): needles drawn locally, the ghost at pair $i$ turned by
$A_y(x_i)\,\Delta y$, in place of the needle accumulated from the left edge.
The accumulated rendering (the current hero's bottom pane and the current pluck
figure) turns every needle to the right of a tap at the instant of the tap,
because the reference needle is transported from the left end; that is a fact
about the reference convention, and it draws an instantaneous action at a
distance inside a figure whose point is lateness. The local rendering is honest
without a confession, and it makes the transverse component the drawn thing
instead of the confessed thing ("I show you one line of the plane").

Not yet understandable at the top: what the curve is, why a row of clocks
carries a rule at all, why a rule has a speed, and what light has to do with
it. Returns at the end with the readout lane the textbook draws — E as the
pulse's time-slope, B as its space-slope, in step — and with the reader knowing
why the rule exists (§1), why it cannot be erased (§3), why it moves (§4), and
what the faint needle's swing is (the disagreement between the rows; the
expected-twist term of $D\theta$; read by a loop straddling the rows as $B$).

Familiarity cost: low. A clock row with a comparison rule drawn under it
appears nowhere in the popular record; the nearest published object is the
current article's own bottom pane. What is spent: the "the textbook arrows were
readouts all along" reversal, which needed the familiar diagram on top. It
returns as the overlay at the end, where seeing the familiar thing after the
unfamiliar one costs nothing.

The current hero is not kept: it opens on the most reproduced figure in
physics, poses no question, and its bottom pane is a draft of this hero drawn

### Thesis

Light is a wave in the rule for comparing one clock with the next, and that
rule exists because, without it, no derivative of a phase means anything.

The title's two nouns are paid as follows: *bundle* — the clock faces over the
line are the fibers, the whole row is the bundle, a wave is a section (§1, on
the clocks, when the words are needed); *universal medium* — the operator with
the unknown swapped (§4's last figure) and the ending's verdict that the medium
is a rule.

### The five sentences

1. A tap on a flat blue curve between two rows of resting clocks raises a bump
   that splits, runs both ways at one fixed speed, and swings each pair's faint
   needle as it passes, later at every pair — and the curve is not a wave value
   but a rule for comparing neighbors.
2. The obvious way to do calculus on a row of clocks is the string's way —
   subtract neighbors' angles as if they were heights — and it fails on screen:
   repaint the zero-marks with a brush and the energy formula jumps while the
   rope it describes never moves.
3. A transport rule $A$, tuned by hand until the mangled wave reads calm, repairs
   the derivative ($D\theta = \partial\theta - A$ holds still under any brush)
   and creates the diary problem: on a line, every such rule is erased by one
   counter-stroke, so the first figure's pulse could be a change of pure
   convention traveling — a time reform running down a telegraph line, which no
   meter reads.
4. On a plane a needle carried by two routes arrives disagreeing; only enclosure
   counts; a shrunk loop gives a density $F$ that no brush can create or
   destroy, and electrons sent around a sealed core slide their stripes by
   exactly the enclosed loop — which makes $A$ physical and leaves it frozen:
   every rule so far was scenery set by hand, and a physical rule that cannot
   change is not yet a physical thing.
5. Given the cheapest life a rule can have — it costs to curve and it costs to
   change, the string's two costs re-aimed at the rule — the first figure's
   pluck obeys $\partial^2 A_y/\partial t^2 = c^2\,\partial^2 A_y/\partial x^2$,
   runs at $c$, and carries two readouts no brush can touch, $E = -\partial_t
   A_y$ and $B = \partial_x A_y$, in step: the two rows are a slice of light,
   and the medium between them is the rule.

### Section ladder

**§1 The string's recipe**

- Naive build: put a wave on the clocks and treat angles as heights. A whirled
  rope, each point on a circle (`RopeCircle` `'rope'`, REUSE); the circle
  lifted out as the clock face at that point (`RopeCircle` `'extract'`, REUSE);
  the clock row with the $\theta(x)$ graph beneath and the string's energy
  recipe as a meter reading 1.00 on a steady wave (`RopeCircle` `'clocks'`,
  REUSE). The words arrive on the clocks as needed: the face is the fiber, the
  row of faces over the line is the bundle, the run of needles is a section. No
  strip, no band. One world-anchor stated cold, no figure: before railway time
  every town's noon was its own, and the Corn Exchange clock in Bristol still
  carries two minute hands ten minutes apart. The zero-marks are paint.
- Visible failure: the regauge brush. The dual meter first (`RegaugeBrush`
  `'pre'`, REUSE): rope-energy from the visible rope, formula-energy from the
  plotted slopes. Prediction prompt — kept, because the common guess, "nothing
  physical changes, so no meter moves," is wrong. Brush the zero-marks
  (`RegaugeBrush` `'brush'`, REUSE): the rope-meter holds; the formula-meter
  jumps; the rope in the top pane never moves. Boundary check in prose: a
  constant $\alpha$ leaves every slope alone; only a place-varying repaint
  breaks the derivative. Verdict: the ordinary derivative of a phase is a fact
  about labels. Then the room and rope removed (`RegaugeBrush` `'stripped'`,
  REUSE): nature's clocks come with no room; no experiment has read one clock's
  absolute angle, only comparisons between clocks.
- Savior: what the row lacks is a rule for carrying a needle from one clock to
  the next — as data, drawn. The first figure has one.
- Knob: time-speed on the clock row; the brush on the regauge figures.
- Math: $E = \int[(\partial_t\theta)^2 + c^2(\partial_x\theta)^2]\,dx$, the
  string's recipe on angles — formalizes the meter the reader watched read 1.00.
  $\theta \to \theta + \alpha(x)$ — formalizes the brushstroke the reader just
  made; $\partial_x\theta \to \partial_x\theta + \partial_x\alpha$ is what the
  meter jumped by.

**§2 The rule**

- Naive build: the rule as data — a needle carried along the row, turned by
  $A(x)\,dx$ per step, $A$ drawn as its own blue curve, $A = 0$ the "don't turn
  it" transport (`TransportNeedle`, REUSE). Then the rule tuned by hand against
  a mangle: one slider (`ConnectionTuner` `'stage1'`, REUSE); three handles
  (`ConnectionTuner` `'stage2'`, REUSE). The raw $\theta$ trace never moves
  while tuning: the rule repairs the reading, not the labels.
- Visible failure: three handles against a many-stroke mangle — after some
  trial and error the reader gets close and no closer; a real brush has as many
  handles as there are points. The name for the number the hand was driving to
  zero: $D\theta = \partial_x\theta - A$. Then the law (`ConnectionTuner`
  `'law'`, REUSE): brush a fresh $\alpha$ over the repaired wave; $A$ absorbs the
  stroke; $D\theta$ sits still; the §1 meter rebuilt from $D\theta$ holds under
  anything painted. Boundary: $A = 0$ recovers the string; and the room's shared
  "up" was $A = 0$, a rule handed free by the scenery — the string never had this
  problem because its rule came with the room.
- Failure driving out (the diary): the scene as the tuner left it and one
  prepared counter-stroke (`DiaryEraser`, REUSE): $\partial\theta$ and $A$ go to
  zero together while $D\theta$ never moves; on a line every tuned $A$ is a
  diary of brushstrokes. Sharpened against the hero: a change of pure convention
  can travel — a time reform running down a telegraph line, town by town, late,
  with no hand moving (`RailwayReform`, REUSE). If the first figure's curve is a
  diary, its pulse is this: a reform, read by no meter.
- Savior: on a line a needle has one way to travel and no way to disagree with
  itself; on a plane it has many.
- Waypoint, the article's one, stated as a black box: two tools go forward — a
  rule $A$ saying what "same direction" means between neighbors, and a
  derivative $D$ that subtracts through it. Downstream figures use them as
  given.
- Knob: one amplitude slider (stage 1); three handles (stage 2, the one
  exception); the brush (law); one counter-stroke slider (eraser); the reform
  front (reform).
- Math: $D\theta = \partial_x\theta - A$ — the reader drove this to zero by hand.
  $\theta \to \theta + \alpha,\ A \to A + \partial_x\alpha,\ D\theta \to D\theta$
  — the reader failed to break this with a brush.

**§3 Loops**

- Naive build: the plane of clocks; $A$ becomes a pair $(A_x, A_y)$, one number
  per direction of step, one blue arrow per clock; carry a needle from P to Q by
  two routes (`HolonomyLoop` `'two-paths'`, REUSE). The arrivals disagree, though
  every step obeyed the rule.
- Visible failure: two routes disagreeing is one loop coming home rotated.
  Prediction prompt — kept, because the common guess, that a loop missing the
  core picks up a smaller rotation, is wrong: one loop around the marked core,
  one not (`HolonomyLoop` `'two-loops'`, REUSE). The enclosing loop's needle
  comes home rotated; the other returns exactly as it left. Free hunt
  (`HolonomyLoop` `'explore'`, REUSE): position changes nothing; only enclosure
  counts. Shrink with two meters (`HolonomyLoop` `'shrink'`, REUSE): halve the
  radius, fourfold drop — rotation per area, $F$ — and the two meters agree at
  every radius. The violet map (`HolonomyLoop` `'field'`, REUSE). The diary test
  (`HolonomyLoop` `'gauge-check'`, REUSE): a pure $A = \partial\alpha$ scores
  zero on every loop at every radius. The brush can move $A$; it cannot create
  or destroy $F$. Anything a loop reads is not diary — §2's failure answered by
  a meter.
- Lab: the same loop with electrons for needles (`ABInterference` `'revealed'`,
  REUSE): two paths around a sealed core, stripes where electrons land, a dial
  setting the enclosed flux — the loop's reading — with no curvature on either
  path; the pattern recurs once per $h/e \approx 4.14\times10^{-15}$ Wb. The
  rope loan is paid here in one paragraph: the clocks are the phase every
  charged particle carries — no room, no visible needle, only comparisons.
  Tonomura, one figure delta (`ABInterference` `'tonomura'`, REUSE): a
  superconducting sheath that traps flux only in units of $h/2e$, so the fringes
  have two options, and the half-stripe is what was found. Loops of $A$ move
  fringes in a laboratory.
- Failure driving out: every rule on this plane, and in the tuner, was
  prescribed and held still — drag the loop anywhere and the rule never
  answers. The first figure's rule answered a tap.
- Savior: a rule that carries a measurable quantity and cannot change is
  scenery, and nothing in nature is scenery. The next act lets go of it.
- Knob: playback (two-paths, two-loops); drag the loop (explore); radius
  (shrink); the dial (AB); the stepped dial (Tonomura).
- Math: $F = \partial_x A_y - \partial_y A_x$ and $\oint A = \iint F$ —
  formalizes the two meters the reader watched agree; the pure-gauge check
  formalizes "the brush cannot make $F$."

**§4 The rule set moving**

- The postulate, in place of a naive build: give the rule the cheapest life on
  offer. It costs energy for the rule to curve and it costs energy for the rule
  to change — the string's two costs, tension and inertia, re-aimed at the rule.
  This is the one thing added by hand. The rider — the cheapest dynamics plays
  no favorites among uniformly moving observers — is relativity's fact, stated
  once and not derived.
- The pluck formalized: the first figure's tap — two fronts at one speed, the
  $ct$ markers — obeys $\partial^2 A_y/\partial t^2 = c^2\,\partial^2
  A_y/\partial x^2$, with the prankster parked (Lorenz gauge, named in one
  clause). The waving quantity is the rule for across-steps between the rows;
  the loop meter from §3, straddling the two rows, reads $\partial_x A_y$ — the
  curvature — and a step in time is a step too, with its own rotation-per-step,
  giving $-\partial_t A_y$.
- Readouts (`ConnectionWave` `'readouts'`, OVERLAY on the new hero rendering:

### Ending

The two rows return with the textbook lane above them (hero + OVERLAY): the two
arrow families every textbook draws for light are the time-slope and the
space-slope of the curve the reader plucked, in step because both are
derivatives of one shape $f(x - ct)$. The faint needles swinging are the two
rows disagreeing about "same," and the disagreement is what travels.

Then the verdict on the medium, from this figure's own parts. Between the two
rows there is nothing with parts and nothing with a rest frame — only the
number that says how far a needle turns on the way up. An instrument built in
1887 to find the wind of that medium was hunting a property a rule does not
have; the medium was not disproved, it was never the kind of thing that could
blow. The measured isotropy — the same speed in every direction the instrument
was turned — is a second fact, relativity's, taken here and not derived.

And the inversion in its honest form: nothing was added to make light except
the cheapest life a rule can have. The rule itself was forced by the brush and
kept by the loop.

Last figure: the hero. No Chern, no Michelson consoled, no next-time-you
benediction, no audit of a quote.

Movability test: the p-bits ending lands "the flipping is the computation" on a
sampler; lesson 01 lands a solver; this one lands "the medium of light is a
comparison rule with no rest frame" on two rows of clocks, and no sibling has a

## Part 3 — The intro

Two rows of clocks: every needle points the same way, and nothing moves.
Between the rows runs a blue curve, flat. The curve is a rule: at each position,
how far a lower needle must turn, carried up to its partner, to still count as
pointing the same way. A flat curve says: not at all, anywhere. Each upper
clock also shows a fainter needle, its partner's carried up by the rule; with the
curve flat, the two coincide.

A tap on the curve raises a bump, and the bump does not stay. It splits, and
each half runs to its end of the rows at one speed, the same for every tap
anywhere. At each pair a half passes, the faint needle swings off
and back — each pair later than the last. Nothing was added between
the rows to carry it. What travels is the rule, changing. Light is a wave in
the rule for comparing one clock with the next.

Hero figure: two rows of clock faces over a flat blue $A_y(x)$ curve, each
upper clock drawing its own needle and a faint one turned by $A_y\,\Delta y$
from its partner's; one knob — a tap on the curve plucks it (leapfrog on $A_y$,
absorbing ends, Courant 0.5, $ct$ markers on the fronts).
