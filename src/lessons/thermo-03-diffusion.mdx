# Diffusion on a Dreaming Machine

The wall of dreaming lattices that closed Part 1 is back, and it has moved
twice. Every chain now runs on a patch of the Z1 fabric Part 2 made real,
and the glyphs have grown — sixty-four pixels apiece, dreamed by the same
clamp–dream–subtract loop, on weights that ship with the page.
Correctness is not this article's fight. A witness row rides the wall —
the surviving instruments from two articles of auditing — and it prints
the same truths under every schedule this page tries; that sameness is the
point — a schedule changes the bill, never the dreams.

What is new is bolted underneath: a running <C k="bill">bill</C>, charged in
the chip's native currency of Gibbs-iteration equivalents. Part 2 ended
holding the price list — a sweep bills one iteration, a readout three
hundred, a reflash 27,300, ninety-one times a readout — with one operating
limit printed beside it: re-flashing stays below about once per second, or
the advantage is gone. The strip under the wall charges every operation at
those rates. Each number on it is a modeled rate built from the papers'
verified constants, and the strip says so on its own face; nothing on any
bill in this article is a measurement of hardware.

Run the wall the way Part 1 built it and the bill is absurd on sight. Each
dream descends its three noise levels; each level is its own kernel; each
kernel swap is a reflash. Three reflashes per dream, 27,300 apiece — the
<C k="bill">reflash line item</C> devours the strip, and the
once-per-second readout burns <C k="ferro">red</C> before the second dream
finishes. Converted to energy at the papers' §II B estimate of roughly
$3\times10^{-10}$ joules per iteration — an estimate, and it is labeled
one — the naive wall pays 27.6 microjoules per dream: 36,264 samples per
joule, printed in error-tint.

The wall returns at the end of this article running the identical model,
dreaming the identical dreams, and that slot prints a different number.
Two flat facts frame everything between. Nothing UNet-shaped survives
compilation onto this machine: the reverse denoiser at each level is the
energy model itself — clamp the evidence, marginalize the help, normalize —
Part 2's thermodynamic kernel with no further ceremony. And everything
stays binary end to end; the continuous, score-matched diffusion of the
image generators never appears in this article.

## The reflash wall

One dream is a short program. Clamp sixteen fair coins as evidence. Sweep
the free spins a handful of times. Read the output back — it is the next
level's evidence. Swap in the next level's kernel. Three times over, and a
glyph has condensed out of static, exactly as it did in Part 1 — except
that every verb in that paragraph now has a price. Sweeps and readouts and
reflashes carry Part 2's rates unchanged. Clamping is the newcomer: the
papers price holding a cell like flashing one, and this article charges it
per node — a reflash spread over the patch's 144 cells, 189.58
iteration-equivalents for every node written. That per-node basis is the
one modeling choice this article adds to Part 2's constants, stated here
once and frozen.

Four line items, then. The sweeps are the machine's whole life — the thing
the fabric does natively, continuously, millions of times a second. The
reflashes are three brief events per dream.

The composition bars call it: 91,918 iteration-equivalents per dream, and

part of the program that dreams — are eighteen of those iterations: two
hundredths of one percent. The dream is a rounding error on its own bill.
The runner-up is worth registering too, because it is not the readouts:
clamping the evidence takes 9.9%, and that item outlives every repair on
this page. Meanwhile the rate line does its own accounting — at the pace
the figure runs, the naive schedule fires 1.80 reflashes per second
against a limit of about one, and the readout burns
<C k="ferro">red</C> the whole time.

The first repair is not a better model. It is a loop order. The naive
loops read: for each dream, for each level, flash that level's kernel. Run
them the other way — every dream through level three before any kernel
swap, then every dream through level two — and a batch of $N$ dreams pays
three reflashes total instead of $3N$. Drag the batch knob and watch the
cost-per-sample curve fall as $1/N$ onto a drawn floor: 10,018 iterations,
the sub-bill with no reflash in it. At a batch of 64 the per-sample cost
lands at 11,298, and the rate readout drops from 1.80 reflashes per second
to 0.200 — a fifth of the limit.

The wall above the curve shows what the reorder spends. At a batch of one,
three dreams had finished inside the first five seconds; at sixty-four,
the first batch is still mid-descent — every dream arrives, and they
arrive together, late. Batching buys reflashes with latency, and the trade
is visible before it is named.

Batching diluted the reflashes; it did not remove them. Three per batch,
forever — and the reason there are three at all is that the level index
lives in the weights, so changing levels means changing the landscape.
Moving that index somewhere cheaper is the next repair.

## Three amortizations

The three kernels of the descent differ only in what they believe about
noise — how much of the evidence to trust, how much to invent. They are
three members of one family, indexed by the level. And this machine has a
native way to feed a kernel an index: clamp it. Give the energy two more
input spins carrying a binary code $\tau_t$ for the level $t \in
\{1,2,3\}$, and train one kernel on all three levels at once:

$$E_\theta(x_t, \tau_t, w, y) \;=\; -\,b\cdot y \;-\; c\cdot w \;-\; [x_t;\tau_t]^{\mathsf T} U\, y \;-\; w^{\mathsf T} W\, y$$

Formally there is nothing new in that line — it is Part 2's thermodynamic
kernel with a wider clamp set, eighteen held spins instead of sixteen.
What continuous diffusion carries as a time-embedding arrives here as two
clamped spins riding beside the evidence. Check the boundary: hold
the code fixed and its rows of $U$ collapse into a constant field on the
outputs — a per-level bias — so the conditioned kernel *is* a specialist
for that level, an identity the check harness confirms on the trained
weights to one part in a billion. The code spins are clamped in every
phase of training and every step of dreaming; they are never sampled. The
papers' own diffusion demonstration on this hardware family — a τ-leaping
chain of independent bit-flip gates — is named here once as lineage; the
chain on this page is a different object, a trained conditional energy
model per level.

Flashed once, this kernel serves the whole descent. Changing levels means
re-clamping two spins, and clamps were already on the bill. The reflash
line item goes to the best value it can have: one flash, ever, amortizing
toward zero as the wall keeps dreaming.

The price is accuracy, and it is measured, not waved at. Against three
specialists trained on the same data stream and budget, the shared
kernel's per-level exact KL — the 4×4 oracle still holds everything on
this page — comes out worse by a factor of 1.20 at the middle level, and
by 4.0 and 7.5 at the ends. Sharing is nearly free exactly where the
task is copy-like, and genuinely costly at the edges — with one asterisk
the oracle insists on: at the noisiest level the target is broad enough
that even an untrained kernel scores 0.90 by pure vagueness, and that
level is honestly the shared kernel's weakest. The code itself carries
real information: feed the kernel the wrong level's code and its KL rises
by about 0.2 nats.

The third amortization spends a different resource entirely. The fabric is
a quarter-million cells and the wall's patch is 144 of them — so flash
all three specialists once, onto three vertex-disjoint patches of the real
graph, and let the descent alternate regions. Zero reflashes after the
three setup flashes. The footprint counter prices the trade the other
way: 432 physical p-bits held, against one patch's 144.

still being paid for, in clamp ink, on every dream. The right-hand bars
hold the other axis of the trade: the disjoint schedule's footprint is
three patches whether or not anyone is dreaming — drag the demand knob and
the fabric bars do not move — and the batched row above both, still
swapping kernels every batch of eight, sits at 20,256.

The divergence ends in a verdict, and the figure prints it: batched +
conditioned is the schedule the rest of this article runs — within 2.5%
of the cheapest at every demand level, on a single patch, one flash ever.
Disjoint is deferred with its regime named rather than dismissed: it wins
when the levels are few and the fabric is idle, and a quarter-million-cell
die has a great deal of idle fabric.

## The clamps

The conditioned schedule bought the best possible reflash line. Price
what remains. The left pane amortizes the one setup flash over the samples
produced — a wedge collapsing as $1/S$ — and what it collapses onto is the
floor: 11,155.5 iterations per sample at the dream default of six sweeps
per level. The right pane takes the floor apart.

<ClampFloor />

The clamps are 10,237.5 of it — 91.8%. Re-clamping the evidence is not
overhead around the algorithm; at this point it is most of what the bill
is. And the one line item a reader can inflate refuses to change the
verdict: drag the sweeps knob two orders of magnitude past the default,
to six hundred sweeps per level, and the clamp share falls only to
79.1% — still the majority of every dream's cost.

There is no schedule that escapes this. Conditioning on evidence is what
a reverse step *is*, and on this machine conditioning is physical: the
evidence $x_t$ must be written into held cells, node by node, at flash
prices, at every level of every sample. Reflashes could be reordered away
because the kernel swap was bookkeeping — the same three landscapes,
shuffled. The clamps cannot, because they are the data entering the
machine. The amortizations did not abolish the bill; they stripped it
down to the part that is the algorithm.

The campaign so far, totaled: 91,918 iterations per dream down to 11,582
at a batch of 64 — a measured factor of 7.94, with 8.24 as the ceiling
once the flash is fully amortized. Everything cheaper than that has to
come out of the floor itself — and the smallest line item on the floor,
eighteen sweeps, is also the only one that decides whether the dreams are
any good.

<Waypoint>
  You can amortize a schedule three ways and call the verdict: reorder the

  all three by name, 91.8% clamps at the default. What the bill cannot
  say is whether six sweeps per level is the right six, or even whether
  the three levels deserve the same six.
</Waypoint>

## The mixing budget

Part 2 priced sweeps in a second currency: the mixing tax, τ sweeps of
waiting per independent sample, inflated exponentially by stiff couplings.
The three kernels of the descent are not equally stiff, and the number is
measurable the same way it was measured then — run each level's chain
long enough and read its autocorrelation time. On the shipped kernels,
from twenty-thousand-sweep chains: <C k="ferro">τ</C> = 1.09, 2.86, 2.05
for the clean, middle, and noisiest levels.

Part 2 also left one exemption on the table, quoted and waiting: clamped
spins do not enter this tradeoff. On the trained denoiser, that line
splits the model down the middle. The couplings <C k="held">U</C> pour
evidence from clamped cells into the outputs — every one of them hangs
off a spin that never flips, so no barrier of their making ever needs
crossing; they can be as violent as training likes at zero mixing cost.
The couplings <C k="hid">W</C> run from the hidden spins to the outputs —
the machinery Part 1 added so that sixteen pixels could conspire — and
they are free-side: everything τ taxes, it taxes there. The falsifiable
form runs in the check harness, where it belongs: double W at the middle
level and τ jumps from 2.86 to 47 — a factor of 16.5; double U at any
level and τ never rises by more than 15%, and at the middle level it
falls. The tax lands entirely on the machinery of coherence; conditioning
rides exempt.

So the sweep budget looks like a lever. Nine sweeps per dream, fixed —
tight enough that where they go should matter — and a knob that morphs
the split from a uniform three-three-three toward τ-proportional, which
at the far end reads two-four-three. Two walls dream side by side at
bills equal to the iteration, same seeds, same clamps, same readouts;
under each wall sit the witnesses, stray rate and distance to the nearest
glyph, because a cheaper schedule claims nothing here unless the quality
witness sits on the same canvas.

The τ bars adjudicate, and they rule against both candidates: 2.05 at the
noisy first step, 1.09 at the nearly-clean last, and the largest — 2.86 —
in the middle, where the dream is half-committed. The measured table is
the fact; neither intuition predicted its shape.

Then the knob delivers the section's real result, which is a null. Move
the split from uniform to τ-weighted and the witnesses refuse to care.
The harness ran four hundred dreams a side: the weighted split does no
better — 0.170 strays against uniform's 0.138. The obvious objection is
that this τ table is too flat to matter — 1.09 to 2.86 is hardly a
spread — so the harness sharpened it on purpose: scale the middle level's
W by 1.75 and its τ explodes to 18.6. Five hundred dreams a side under
that handicap: uniform 0.184, weighted 0.180. A null both ways.

The null is the finding, and it has a mechanism worth stating exactly. τ
is a stationary quantity — it prices how long a chain running at
equilibrium takes to forget where it was. The dreams never run that
chain. Each reverse step starts warm, clamped to the previous level's
output, already inside the neighborhood it needs to search, and takes
two to four sweeps rather than hundreds. A warm-started short run never
buys the decorrelation that τ prices, so it never pays for it either:
warm-started reverse steps do not pay τ at this size. That sentence is a
measurement, not a theorem, and it is scoped to what was measured —
sixteen pixels, nine sweeps, these kernels. The schedule's last free
parameter turns out, here, to be genuinely free: moving it moves nothing
the witnesses can see.

## Past the oracle's edge

The obvious move is to add pixels, and it dies twice before anything runs.
An 8×8 glyph is sixty-four spins, and sixty-four spins have $2^{64}$ states —
the exact enumeration behind every <C k="ghost">gray ghost</C> since Part 1's
atlas would need on the order of $10^{19}$ additions, and no figure will ever
carry one again. The second death is quieter. A bigger picture needs richer
conspiracies among its pixels, and the reflex is to wire pixels to pixels —
denser couplings for a denser image. The fabric never offers the chance.

Choose instead *which* sixty-four cells of a 16×16 fabric patch are the
pixels, and take them all from one checkerboard color — the same one-color
condition Part 2's layer figure quietly relied on. Then hang the rest of the
patch from them by wiring distance, exactly the re-hang you have now watched
twice as a curiosity. This time it is the model. The 256-cell patch falls
into three banks — the 64 pixels, then 128 hidden cells one wire away, then
64 more behind those — and all 2,048 wires of the patch land between
adjacent banks. Part 1 called this hanging an architecture nobody designed.
It has now designed ours: a deep, layered Boltzmann machine, its depth and
widths dictated by nothing but where sixty-four squares sit on the die.

And look at what the one-color choice did to the reflex we started with.
Every wire in this fabric joins opposite checkerboard colors, and every
pixel is the same color — so the number of pixel-to-pixel wires is exactly
zero. Whatever makes two patches of ink agree across the glyph must route
through the hidden banks, because no direct route exists. Part 1 showed
pixels-that-cannot-conspire as a *failure* — the factorized dreams, half a
box welded to half a checker — and the repair was a small hidden bank all
pixels consult. The fabric turns that repair into the only grammar on
offer: 192 hidden spins are not a design choice here, they are everything
the patch contains that is not a pixel. Per noise level the model owns
2,048 couplings on real wires, 256 biases, and 64 input wires — one held
wire per pixel, carrying the evidence $x_t$ in — 2,368 numbers, three times
over for the three levels.

Here it is dreaming. Left, the patch itself: squares are pixels, faint dots
the hidden banks, everything flickering under the same chromatic sweeps as
always. Right, the filmstrip — coins at $x_3$, a glyph at $x_0$ — with the
frame currently being consumed as clamped evidence wearing the
<C k="held">green halo</C>. The glyph family is new, seven members at 8×8,
no two closer than twenty pixels apart. The weights ship on credit one last
time, and this time the debt settles by receipt rather than re-performance:
the paint-box wall at 4×4 keeps the live training job, while this model
trained offline in forty-five seconds, seven hundred epochs per level, and
the shipped constants match a fresh run of that stated recipe to five parts
in a million — an identity this article's check harness re-verifies on
every run.

Where the ghost bars used to be, the canvas prints an epitaph instead:
*joint oracle dead*. Part 1's last page promised what happens next — the
oracle "degrades into a hierarchy of witnesses, each with a smaller
jurisdiction" — and the meter row under the filmstrip is that promise doing
its job. Three witnesses keep live tallies on the canvas as dreams finish;
a fourth ticks offstage; and the shipped model's full audit — 250 dreams
and a deeper probe set, run in the same harness that re-verifies the
weights — puts a number on each. The **fenced conditional**: inside a 2×2
pixel window with its surroundings held, the exact reverse conditional is
still computable, and the trained model sits 0.053 from it in total
variation at the gentlest level, 0.178 at the middle one — against 0.937
untrained. The **pinned moments**: the pairwise correlations of the 250
dreams miss the glyph family's by 0.117, versus 0.343 before training, with
183 of the 250 landing within ten pixels of a family member and no glyph
left undreamed. The **known-answer probe**: wreck a known glyph with the
forward noise, run the descent on the wreck, and 15.63 pixels astray come
back as 2.78. And the **autocorrelation clock**: the visible magnetization
of the last level's chain decorrelates in 5.7 sweeps — the going rate, in
Gibbs time, for one independent look at what this chain believes. No single
one of these is the oracle. Together they are what trusting a sampler means
at any size the world actually uses — and they still catch old crimes: run
this same chain on the stale-field synchronous schedule and the moments
witness moves by 0.063, the write-conflict offense from Part 1's big
lattice convicted at a scale its meter never reached. One warranty from
Part 2 rides along unchanged: the chain's drift still saturates at the
depth-independent floor $\tilde\delta \le \bar\varepsilon/(1-\rho)$,
because contraction never cared how many spins were doing the contracting.

The knob deserves a farewell reading. At sixteen pixels, the allocation
measurement came back a shrug — split the sweep budget any way you like,
the dreams barely notice. At sixty-four, the shrug is gone: at four sweeps
per level the finished dreams sit a mean 12.01 pixels from the nearest
family member, at twenty-four they sit at 4.95. The coherence is bought
with Gibbs time, the mixing tax finally presenting at figure scale — and
each further notch of scale moves the sweep line of the bill the same
direction, from rounding error toward rent.

## The ceiling

Fabric has been spent in this article before — the disjoint-region schedule
bought its zero reflashes with 432 p-bits against the conditioned kernel's
144. The model above spends 256, one patch, pixels and hidden banks
included. The remaining question is what spending the *whole die* buys, and
for one recipe the papers pin the answer to an anchor: a 16×16 field of
8-bit pixels, embedded their way, costs about 14,000 p-bits.

Both answers are on the chart, and the distance between them is the honest
finding. The curves scale the anchor naively — p-bits proportional to area
times bit depth — and the 8-bit curve crosses the 250,000 ceiling at
$16\sqrt{250000/14000} \approx 67.6$ on a side, computed live from the two
verified constants and nothing else. The papers themselves claim 32×32 to
48×48, the <C k="ghost">gray band</C>. The naive curve runs optimistic
because its one assumption is flat overhead per pixel, and minor-embedding
chains lengthen as fields grow; the band is the paper's own number. So the
chart plots the band as the claim and the crossing as the arithmetic, and
lets the gap between them stand as the measured price of that assumption.
Two more readings from the same constants: at one bit per pixel the naive
crossing runs to 191 on a side, and our entire 8×8 model — the one you just
watched dream — is a dot at 256 p-bits, about one-thousandth of the die.

## The billed wall of dreams

The wall is back for the third and last time — the forty lattices that
opened Part 1 wearing static, that closed it dreaming your painted glyph,
and that opened this article with a bill it could not defend. It runs here
under the settled schedule: batched, conditioned, the sweep budget split
evenly across levels because the allocation measurement said the levels do
not yet care. Hold it against the opening figure and the first thing to
check is what did not move: the dreams. The sample stream is bit-identical
under both schedules — same seeds, same kernels, same sweeps — because
reordering loops and sharing one flashed landscape changes when operations

same thing in its own language, panes matching Part 1's trained quality
throughout. The schedule changes the bill, and only the bill.

## Further Reading

The Thermalizers paper (arXiv:2608.01615) supplied this article's spine
twice over, and its appendices are the part to read now. Appendix K is the
14,000-p-bit anchor's home — fixed-point registers assembled from chains of
p-bits, a compiled Gaussian posterior held against exact inference — and
Appendix B is the energy accounting every bill in this article modeled its
rates from. Its most imitable habit is the one the cost strips borrowed: it
keeps its estimates and its measurements in visibly different registers,
and never lets one impersonate the other.

The Torx paper (arXiv:2608.01612) carries the acknowledged ancestor of this
finale: a discrete-diffusion chain on binary MNIST, run as τ-leaping over
gate programs — independent bit-flip gates first, then coupled ones, with
the coupling buying back a measured share of the quality the independent
gates leave on the table. Their chain and ours differ in machinery and
agree in thesis — a diffusion model for this hardware is a program of
kernels, judged by what coupling buys — and reading their version after
building this one is the fastest way to see which choices were forced and
which were ours.

THRML and the Torx toolkit remain the public, runnable half of the stack,
and for this article's questions in particular they are the natural next
instrument: every amortization staged above is, in the end, a loop you can
reorder yourself, at sizes where the bill arrives as wall-clock time
instead of a modeled strip.

Sohl-Dickstein, Weiss, Maheswaranathan, and Ganguli's 2015 paper, *Deep
Unsupervised Learning using Nonequilibrium Thermodynamics*, is where
diffusion models began, and where they began is the point: the
destroy-then-learn-the-undo loop this series trained three times was
derived there from the statistical physics of diffusion itself, years
before the architectures that made it famous. A decade of engineering
later, this lesson ran the same loop on a machine whose native act is
thermal relaxation. Read it last and the whole series folds into one
sentence of history: the idea came from thermodynamics, went to the GPUs,
and here it is being fitted for the trip home.

## Final Words

The wall dreaming at the top of Part 1 was forty distributions wearing
static. The same wall, closing this page, reads three levels deep, one
level per article: the Boltzmann law each
lattice dwells in, the compiled program each descent executes, the bill
each dream leaves behind. Nothing was added to the wall in three articles —
layers of reading were.

Pricing the dreams did not shrink them. The strays on the wall are still
strays, the glyphs still condense out of static unannounced, and the one
line of the bill that survives every amortization is the clamps — the cost
of holding the evidence still while a picture forms around it.

Somewhere right now a building of GPUs is dreaming pictures against a bill
nobody prints; this page printed one, small and modeled and confessed line
by line. Part 1 closed its atlas on a fact read at two scales — the local
rule and the global law, one object. The series closes on that fact's
sequel, one level up: the schedule and the bill are one object read twice,
once by the fabric, which executes it, and once by whoever pays.
